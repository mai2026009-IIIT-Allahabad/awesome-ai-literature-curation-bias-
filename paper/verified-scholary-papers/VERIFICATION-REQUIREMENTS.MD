# Bias Toward Highly Cited and English-Language Papers in AI Literature Curation

> A curated collection of research papers exploring citation bias,
> language bias, and the representation of research in AI-assisted
> literature curation.

---

## 📌 About

AI systems and Large Language Models (LLMs) are increasingly being used
to search, recommend, summarize, and curate scientific literature.

This raises an important question:

> **Do AI literature-curation systems give equal visibility to relevant
> research, or do they reinforce existing biases such as citation and
> English-language bias?**

This repository collects and organizes research that helps explore this
question.

The collection includes research on:

- Citation and publication bias
- English-language and linguistic bias
- AI and LLM bias
- Literature recommendation
- Systematic-review screening
- Citation analysis
- Research evaluation
- Methods and benchmarks for studying these problems

---

## 🎯 Purpose

The goal of this repository is to provide a simple starting point for
students, researchers, and developers interested in understanding bias
in AI-assisted literature discovery and curation.

The repository focuses on three main questions:

1. **Citation Bias**  
   Are highly cited papers more likely to receive visibility or
   recommendation?

2. **Language Bias**  
   Are English-language or standard-English papers more likely to be
   discovered, included, or represented?

3. **AI/LLM Influence**  
   Can AI-based literature systems reproduce or amplify existing
   biases in scientific publishing and discovery?

## 📄 Information Included for Each Paper

Each paper entry contains enough information for another researcher to
understand why the paper belongs in this collection.

Typically, each entry includes:

- **Paper title**
- **Authors**
- **Publication year**
- **Journal / Conference**
- **Category**
- **Why the paper is relevant**
- **Official paper link**
- **DOI**, when available

We avoid listing a paper as a bare URL. Each paper should provide
context explaining its relevance to the repository.

## 🔎 Verification

Every paper should be checked before being added to the collection.

Verification includes checking information such as:

- Correct title
- Correct authors
- Publication year
- Journal or conference
- DOI, when available
- Whether the paper genuinely exists
- Whether the paper is relevant to the repository

### Verification Requirements

The complete requirements and verification guidelines are available here:

**→ [VERIFICATION_REQUIREMENTS.md](VERIFICATION_REQUIREMENTS.md)**

Please read this file before adding a new paper.

> **Core rule:** Do not accept a paper simply because an AI tool
> generated or recommended it. Verify the paper using a reliable source.

---

## 🌐 Preferred Sources

When possible, use reliable and authoritative sources such as:

- Publisher websites
- Journal websites
- Conference websites
- DOI records
- Google Scholar
- Semantic Scholar
- PubMed / PubMed Central
- ACL Anthology
- arXiv

A ResearchGate page can be useful for discovering a paper, but an
official publisher, journal, conference, DOI, or repository record
should be preferred for verification whenever available.

## 🤝 Contributing

Found a relevant paper?

You are welcome to contribute.

Before submitting a paper:

1. Check whether it is already in the repository.
2. Identify the appropriate category.
3. Verify the bibliographic information.
4. Explain why the paper is relevant.
5. Add the paper using the required format.
6. Follow the requirements in
   **[VERIFICATION_REQUIREMENTS.md](VERIFICATION_REQUIREMENTS.md)**.
